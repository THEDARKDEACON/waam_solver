# Independent validation on Yanala, Andreucci & Lambiase 2026 — pre-registration
(written 11 Nov 2026, before any calibration or validation run on these beads)

## Data (never used in this project before)
Yanala, Andreucci & Lambiase, Int. J. Adv. Manuf. Technol. 145 (2026) 4999–5019,
doi 10.1007/s00170-026-18642-0, Table 4: 24 single beads, full factorial
P ∈ {2.9, 3.3, 3.5} kW (measured mean of V(t)·I(t)) × v ∈ {300, 450, 600, 750} mm/min
× SOD ∈ {8, 12} mm. ER70S-6 Ø1.0 mm, Sw = 171.4 mm/s, Ar + 18 % CO₂, CV source,
low-carbon steel plate 150 × 30 × 3.3 mm. Measured: bead height H, width,
contact length (bead–substrate interface width), cross-section area, dilution.
Penetration p from dilution = p/(h+p) → p = dil·h/(1−dil).

## Model (fixed in advance)
`jobs/examples/lambiase2026/runNN.yaml`, generated by `tools/make_lambiase_jobs.py`
from `bead_park_measured.yaml`: honest energy split, Goldak volume fixed to the
torch (a_f 2.0, a_r 3.8, c 2.6 mm, ff/fr 0.6/1.4 as Park), dx 0.4 mm.
Heat input η·P with P as published. Mean current not published → 195 A
estimated from the wire melting rate (forces only). Globular droplets at 2.9 kW,
spray at 3.3/3.5 kW (paper's description). Window x 24–28 mm; run time
44 mm / v (torch 26 mm past the window, so the window has frozen).

## Comparison mapping
model W_mm (fusion width at the plate face) ↔ contact length; model D_mm ↔ p;
model H_frozen_mm ↔ bead height. Model W_bead_mm ↔ width (reported, not fitted).

## Split
* Calibrate (η, Goldak b) on 4 corner beads: runs 2, 4, 10, 12
  (2.9/3.5 kW × 450/750 mm/min, SOD 8). Priors η [0.65, 0.95], b [2, 4] mm;
  n = 5 Latin-hypercube samples, seed 23 (jobs/calibration/sweep_lambiase.yaml).
* Predict 14 held-out beads at the best fit (no draws): runs 3, 6, 7, 8, 11
  (SOD 8) and 14, 15, 16, 18, 19, 20, 22, 23, 24 (SOD 12).
* Excluded: v = 300 mm/min (runs 1, 5, 9, 13, 17, 21) — the paper reports this
  speed outside the stable window (bead misalignment).

## Pass criteria (held-out beads, best-fit prediction)
1. Mean absolute error ≤ 10 % for bead height, ≤ 15 % for contact length,
   ≤ 25 % for penetration.
2. Trends: the model reproduces the measured sign of (a) dH/dv, (b) d(contact)/dv,
   (c) d(contact)/dP, averaged over the held-out set.
3. Physical consistency: best-fit η within 0.70–0.90.
Reference scatter in the data (SOD 8 vs 12 pairs, same P and v): H 1 %,
contact length 12 %, penetration 7 %, width 7 % mean relative difference.

The result is reported whatever it is; nothing is re-tuned on held-out beads.

## Amendment 1 (11 Nov 2026, 05:45 — after calibration, before any held-out result was read)
Checking Table 4 against the paper's definitions shows that **contact length exceeds
bead width in 23 of 24 rows** (CL/width 1.23–1.67; only run 13 has CL < width),
although the paper defines contact length as "the width of the bead–substrate
interface measured at the substrate plane", which cannot exceed the bead width.
The transcription was re-checked against the table image and is correct. The rest of
the table is consistent with wire-mass conservation: the reinforcement area
A·(1−dilution) matches the wire supply π/4·d²·Sw/v to within ~10 % on every row.
Which column is the true interface width cannot be settled from the paper.

Consequences, fixed now:
* The pre-registered criteria stay as written and are reported as the primary result.
* One secondary check is added: model bead width `W_bead_mm` (unfitted; read from
  the `RESULT` lines in `runs/calibration/sweep_lambiase.log`) against the
  "width" column. Pass: MAE ≤ 15 % on the 14 held-out beads. Reported alongside the
  primary result, not in place of it.
* A contact-length failure will be reported together with this inconsistency, and
  no claim will be built on the contact-length comparison either way.

Reading:
* The model predicts the direction of every effect and the magnitude of height,
  penetration and bead width to ~12–23 % on 14 beads it never saw, with a
  physically plausible efficiency (0.82) — in contrast to Park (η_eff 1.0).
* Height misses its limit by 1.8 points; the errors are mostly ±10–20 % without a
  single systematic sign (bias +3.6 %), consistent with run-to-run scatter
  (run 22 H 2.86 vs its neighbours ~3.5) at the speed cap (mach fraction up to 0.37).
* Width responds to power far too weakly (≈ 0.3–0.5 mm/kW vs 1.6–2.1 measured):
  the wetting/spreading of a hotter pool on the plate is under-resolved, which is
  also the likeliest source of the −20 % contact-length bias (if that column is a
  width at all — Amendment 1).
* Nothing here was re-tuned. Next independent step: repeats per bead (scatter) and
  a model change for power-driven spreading, re-tested on new data.
